# Shri Radhe Radhe Restaurant
## Owner User Guide

This guide is written for the restaurant owner. You do not need to know programming or edit any technical files. The website and the owner area are designed to be operated through normal buttons, forms, and menus.

## 1. What the website includes

The customer website is the premium public-facing experience. Customers can view the restaurant story, signature dishes, dining highlights, opening hours, location details, place an order, request a reservation, and sign in to save their information.

The owner area is the private restaurant workspace. It is used to review incoming orders, update order progress, see reservation requests, and manage dishes and visual content.

| Area | What it is used for |
|---|---|
| **Customer website** | Customers browse dishes, read about the restaurant, order food, and request tables. |
| **Operations** | Staff review new orders, update order status, and review reservations. |
| **Content Manager** | The owner adds dishes, sets prices, edits descriptions, chooses categories, manages availability, and selects where dish imagery appears. |
| **Profile** | Customers manage their own saved delivery information after signing in. |

## 2. Signing in

Open the restaurant website and choose the sign-in option. Follow the sign-in screen and use the authorized restaurant account. Only the restaurant owner or approved staff account should be given access to the private operations and content areas.

If you see a message saying that access is restricted, the account is not an owner account. Do not try to work around this message. Ask the project administrator to approve the correct restaurant account.

## 3. Managing dishes

Open the **Content Manager** from the private owner area. To add a dish, choose **Add a dish** or **Create menu item** and fill in the visible fields.

| Field | What to enter |
|---|---|
| **Dish name** | The name customers should see, such as Royal Dal Makhani. |
| **Description** | A short, appetizing explanation of the dish. Mention important ingredients. |
| **Price** | Enter the price in Indian rupees without adding symbols if the field asks for a number. |
| **Category** | Choose or enter a useful grouping such as House classics, From the fire, or Desserts. |
| **Dietary label** | Use labels such as Vegetarian, Vegan, or Contains dairy when appropriate. |
| **Dish image** | Choose the dish image using the image control provided in the owner area. |
| **Image placement** | Choose whether the image belongs on a menu card, in the hero story, or in the dining ambience section. |

When the information is ready, choose **Add to menu**. The dish will become available for customers according to its availability setting.

Use the menu list to review dishes. Remove a dish only when it should no longer be shown. If a dish is temporarily unavailable, use its availability control rather than deleting it, so the dish details can be reused later.

## 4. Prices and availability

Review prices whenever the restaurant changes its menu. Keep prices consistent between the printed menu, the customer website, and any in-store signage.

When an item is sold out, mark it unavailable as soon as possible. When it becomes available again, turn it back on. This prevents customers from ordering dishes the kitchen cannot prepare.

Before saving a change, check the dish name, price, description, category, dietary information, and image. A second person can review important price changes before they are published.

## 5. Managing images and the premium appearance

Use clear, well-lit images that represent the real food and restaurant atmosphere. Avoid blurry pictures, screenshots, watermarks from unrelated businesses, or images that do not belong to the restaurant.

Choose image placement carefully. Use the hero placement for the strongest restaurant image, dining placement for ambience and interiors, and menu placement for a specific dish. Keep the visual style consistent: warm lighting, clean composition, and a premium presentation.

If the owner area provides an image preview, review the crop on both a phone-sized view and a desktop-sized view before saving. The most important subject should remain visible in the center of the image.

## 6. Receiving and processing orders

Open **Operations** to view the incoming order queue. New orders appear with an order number, customer name, fulfillment type, total, and current status.

Use the order status controls in this order:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **New** | The restaurant has received the order but has not confirmed it. |
| **Confirmed** | The restaurant has accepted the order. |
| **Preparing** | The kitchen is preparing the food. |
| **Ready** | The order is ready for pickup or handover to delivery staff. |
| **Completed** | The customer has received the order. |
| **Cancelled** | The order will not be fulfilled. |

For the zero-cost launch, payment is handled without storing card details. Delivery uses cash on delivery, and pickup uses pay at pickup. Confirm the payment and fulfillment details with the customer when necessary.

If an order looks incorrect, do not change the total manually without confirming the customer’s request. Contact the customer using the details shown in the order and record the agreed outcome in the restaurant’s normal operating process.

## 7. Handling reservations

Reservation requests appear in the **Reservations** section of Operations. Review the guest name, phone number, requested date, requested time, and party size.

Call the guest to confirm availability. A request is not the same as a confirmed reservation until the restaurant has accepted it. Keep the restaurant’s table plan and the website information aligned.

## 8. Security rules for the owner

Keep the owner account private. Do not share the owner login with customers, delivery workers, or unapproved staff. If another employee needs access, request a separate approved staff account rather than sharing credentials.

Never send passwords, session links, payment information, or private customer details through public messaging groups. The website is intentionally designed so that card numbers and security codes are not stored in the restaurant system.

Use only the secure website address. If the browser shows a security warning, stop and report it instead of entering account or customer information.

## 9. Daily operating checklist

At opening, confirm that the restaurant hours and available dishes are correct. During service, watch the new-order queue and update statuses promptly. Mark sold-out dishes unavailable. Review reservation requests and call guests for confirmation. At closing, make sure completed and cancelled orders have the correct final status.

## 10. Getting help

If a button does not respond, a dish does not appear, or an order seems stuck, take a screenshot of the visible problem and note the approximate time. Do not edit technical files or change hidden settings. Use the owner dashboard for normal work and ask the project administrator for technical assistance when needed.

> The owner should be able to operate the restaurant website through the visual dashboard. No programming, code editing, or server maintenance should be required for normal menu, image, order, or reservation work.
